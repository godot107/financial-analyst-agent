# How liquid is JPMorgan?

**Short answer: the standard liquidity ratios cannot be computed for JPMorgan from this filing, so no ratio-based answer is possible.**

## Why the ratios are missing

- **Current ratio** — current assets over current liabilities, i.e. whether the next year's bills can be covered: not available (doesn't apply: the balance sheet isn't split into current and non-current and there is no operating income line, as is usual for banks and insurers)
- **Quick ratio** — the stricter test using cash, short-term investments and receivables against current liabilities: not available (doesn't apply: the balance sheet isn't split into current and non-current and there is no operating income line, as is usual for banks and insurers)
- **Cash flow ratio** — operating cash flow over current liabilities, the liquidity thrown off by the business itself: not available (doesn't apply: the balance sheet isn't split into current and non-current and there is no operating income line, as is usual for banks and insurers)

All three fail for the same structural reason: a bank's balance sheet is not split into current and non-current items, and there is no operating income line. This is a limitation of the data, not a finding about the firm, and there is no substitute measure here — I am not going to improvise one.

## What the filing does say

The filing describes how liquidity is governed rather than giving a ratio I can reproduce. It states that an independent Liquidity Risk Management function provides oversight of liquidity risk across the firm [P251], and that liquidity governance runs through the Firmwide and regional ALCOs, the Treasurer Committee and the CTC Risk Committee, with the Board Risk Committee recommending liquidity risk tolerances, strategy and policy to the Board [P254]. A Contingency Funding Plan sets out strategies and identifies alternative contingent funding and liquidity resources for a stress period [P258]. The risk factors flag that operations could be impaired by constrained liquidity, that the parent depends on subsidiaries for funding, and that credit-rating downgrades could affect liquidity and funding cost [P589].

## Limits

No prior-year comparison is possible, since the metrics do not compute in either year. There is no peer or industry comparison in this data — only the company against itself.
